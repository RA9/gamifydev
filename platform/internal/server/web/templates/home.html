How it works

  1. 1

    Sit the placement test

    A short diagnostic across the core topics. It decides where you start — not whether you're allowed in.

  2. 2

    Get your path

    Score well and you choose your direction. Fall below the line and Computer Science Foundations is compulsory first — because the rest doesn't stick without it.

  3. 3

    Join a cohort

    Up to {{ index .Data "cohortMax" }} learners on the same path, one shared schedule, and a daily standup. You are not doing this alone, and you are not doing it whenever you feel like it.

  4. 4

    Clear the checkpoints

    Every course ends in one. You write real code, it runs in a sandbox against tests written for that course, and you get the result in seconds. Pass and the next course opens. There is no way to skip it.

What it asks of you

Worth reading before you sign up rather than after.

  • Show up on scheduled days. Post your standup, or get your scheduled work done. Either counts.
  • Can't make it? Say so. File an absence notice and the day is excused — {{ index .Data "excusedBudget" }} of them per cohort, enough for illness, exams and travel.
  • Going quiet is the problem, not being away. {{ index .Data "missedLimit" }} scheduled days of silence and you leave the cohort. Weekends and rest days don't count against you.
  • Leaving isn't a ban. You can reapply after {{ index .Data "reapplyDays" }} days and join a fresh cohort. The point is a group that moves together, not a punishment.

Places are finite and it costs nothing to take one. These rules exist so the people who want a seat can have it.
