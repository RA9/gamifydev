About

Why it works this way.

Most free courses are abandoned, and not because the material is bad. Structure is the part that's expensive to provide and the part that actually decides who finishes.

The decisions, and why

It isn't self-paced

Self-paced is the kindest-sounding constraint and the one most people fail. A cohort on a shared schedule with a daily standup means someone notices when you disappear. That is most of the value, and it is the reason attendance is a rule rather than a suggestion.

Machines grade the code

Every checkpoint runs your program in a sandbox against tests written for that course, and answers in seconds. That isn't a compromise on mentor review — it's better for this job. Feedback the same minute you wrote the bug is worth more than a considered paragraph three days later, and it means the program doesn't stall behind one person's availability. Some checks are hidden until you pass them, so the specification can't be read off the failure list.

You can't skip ahead

Each course is locked until the previous checkpoint passes. It is possible to watch a whole curriculum go by without being able to write any of it, and gates are how we stop that from happening quietly.

The placement test decides where you start

Not whether you're allowed in. Score below the line on the core topics and Computer Science Foundations comes first — data structures, complexity, algorithms, and how the machine underneath actually works. Nothing built on top of a gap in those holds up.

What this isn't, yet

Worth saying plainly, because the previous version of this page claimed otherwise. There are no certificates. There are no live competitions running. There is no job board. Java and JavaScript checkpoints are read by a person rather than graded automatically, so they take longer.

The curriculum is real and readable in full before you sign up — browse it and judge for yourself.
